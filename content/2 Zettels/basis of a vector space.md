A **basis** of $\mathbb{V}$ is a set of [[2 Zettels/linearly independent vectors\|linearly independent vectors]] that [[2 Zettels/linear span of a set of vectors\|span]] $\mathbb{V}$. The number of vectors in a **basis** of a [[vector space\|vector space]] $\mathbb{V}$ equals to $\text{dim}(\mathbb{V})$. Mathematically, let $v_{1}, v_{2},\ldots, v_{n}\in\mathbb{V}$ be $n$ linearly independent vectors. Then, they are said to be a **basis** for $\mathbb{V}$ iff, for any $v\in\mathbb{V}$, there exists $n$ scalars/coefficients $\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{n}$ such that,

$$
v=\beta_{1}v_{1}+\beta_{2}v_{2} + \ldots + \beta_{n}v_{n}
$$

A nice corollary to this is that the representation of a vector in terms of a basis is _unique_.
